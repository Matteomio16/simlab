## October 28, 2026

- Some news stories were being counted twice, for example the same rally reported on two different days. Since
  October 26 we have been taking the copies out, and from today all the ones we found are gone; from October 29, new
  copies are caught as they come in. The effect is small: Kansas moves about 0.2 points toward the Democrat, and no
  other race moves by more than 0.05. Both versions are scored.

## October 27, 2026

- From October 20 to 23, the service running one of our language models gave faulty answers that overstated how
  likely people were to vote. We asked those questions again, and today's forecast is the first to use the corrected
  answers. Earlier days stay as published.

## October 14, 2026

- Polls no longer move the forecast. Each race's starting point was set on October 12 from its polls and
  fundamentals; since then, every change comes from how voter personas react to the news. Polls and prediction markets
  are now used only to check us, every Sunday.
- Because the polls' daily correction is gone, Texas moved on the switch: Talarico now wins 59 in 100 simulated
  elections, up from 52. No other race moved by more than a point, and Republicans hold 50+ seats in 42 in 100, down
  from 44. Both versions of the forecast are scored.

## October 12, 2026

- Daily forecasts begin, every Senate race and the House, updated once a day.

## October 11, 2026

- The methods page now says plainly that each voter persona is a language model, and how many real people's answers
  sit behind the profiles.
- Two jobs listed on the methods page were not running yet, a weekly audit and a second reader for reactions and
  labels, so we took them off the list.
- This page and the track record page are now public. From October 12 the forecast code is frozen: any change to how
  the forecast is made is listed here with its date, and the old and new versions are both scored.
