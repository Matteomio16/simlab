Round 1 and 2 were dots without a hook. Here each direction gets one idea you can say in a sentence, 3 or 4 versions of it, and the same tests: 32 and 16 px, a circle crop, paper and indigo, the lockup with the wordmark, then in context below. Pick one or two to take to final.

One sentence538's fox, the NYT needle: each mark carries a story you can retell. "A person made of voters" beats "a grid".
A silhouette, not a detailPolymarket, Kalshi and The Economist read as a blob at 16 px. Detail only rewards people who already know you.
Own a colourThe Economist's red box, Kalshi's green. Ours is purple, the colour neither party owns.
It can moveThe fox had poses; the needle twitched all night. A mark that can react gets reused in every post.
It shows the methodOur World in Data's plain box says "reference". A mark made of voters says "we simulate people".
My read
  1. Logo: B2, the swing N. It is a letter, so it works as a monogram the way the NYT's T does. It shows the method in one look: voters crossing from one column to the other and changing colour on the way. And it is the only candidate that makes the purple mean something: purple is what blue turns into on its way to red.
  2. Character: A2, Nota, inside the posts rather than as the logo. At profile size a head-and-shoulders figure on a circle looks like the default "no photo" avatar. In posts, leaning, rising or fading with the news, it becomes our 538 fox.
  3. Pie and overlap: clear, but they read as a chart icon and a Venn diagram. C1 is the most striking of the two at large size; neither holds up in the feed.
