## 2. Integrity Index — detailed notes

**What it is.** integrityindex.us is the flagship tool of the Political Integrity Project (politicalintegrity.us), founded in 2025 by Nico Agosta and Daniel Lobo-Lewis. It's a database that grades every member of Congress and 2026 candidate on money in politics — fundraising sources, PAC contributions, stock trades, and conflicts of interest — pulling from FEC, OGE, and congress.gov data ([Our Mission](https://integrityindex.us/mission)). The org also runs Trump Tracker (cabinet financial holdings) and a donor-pledge campaign, and is legally structured as a Hybrid Committee PAC — i.e., it is explicitly a political money operation, not a nonprofit newsroom or academic project ([politicalintegrity.us](https://politicalintegrity.us/)).

**Presenter/format.** The public face is Nico Agosta's personal TikTok/Instagram/YouTube brand "Stocking the Capitol," which functions as the marketing arm for the Index. His videos are on-camera, direct-to-lens explainers — e.g. a "data dump" format where he narrates findings while the on-screen text credits FEC, OpenSecrets and Quiver Quantitative as sources — not voice-over-only ([TikTok](https://www.tiktok.com/@stocking_the_capitol)). Tone is casual and emoji-heavy, with major-announcement framing for product updates. The companion Substack, "Stocking the Capitol," carries longer updates in the same conversational, donor-thanking voice ([Stocking the Capitol](https://stockingthecapitol.substack.com/p/big-updates-to-the-integrity-index)).

**Graphics style.** The web tool itself leans utilitarian/dashboard: an interactive 2026 midterm map with pledge-takers by state and side-by-side candidate comparisons ([2026 Midterm Map](https://integrityindex.us/races)), a zip-code lookup, and (per their own December 2025 changelog) a "mini game" element. When checked directly, the `/scorecards` page returned a disabled-feature message rather than live content — a reminder that half-shipped features left visible in production undercut credibility instantly, worth actively avoiding.

**Cadence & engagement.** Substack posts appear roughly every 1–2 weeks; short-video posting is much more frequent. There's a real gap between channels: one December 2025 Substack update showed only ~57 shares/7 likes/7 comments directly on the post, while the TikTok/IG personal brand carries hundreds of thousands of followers and millions of cumulative likes — short-video reach does not automatically transfer into owned-newsletter engagement.

**Credibility signals claimed.** The site states its methodology is "applied identically to every official, regardless of party" ([politicalintegrity.us](https://politicalintegrity.us/)); it is also free with no paywall, and says it is funded by small donors rather than corporate PAC money.

**Credibility problem to note.** A public discussion thread on the Index observed that its own top-10 highest-scoring leaderboard consisted entirely of politicians from the left wing of the Democratic party, questioning whether the scoring formula (which favors small-dollar and union money) is neutral in effect even if applied by a fixed rule ([Tildes discussion](https://tildes.net/~society/1s2e/integrity_index_of_us_congress_members)). Combined with its PAC status, donate/merch/pledge CTAs embedded in content, and a partisan-skewing output, Integrity Index reads as an advocacy operation with a data veneer rather than a neutral forecaster — the opposite of the methodology-first, non-poll positioning Matteo wants for his own forecast. It is a strong format reference (presenter + cited data + interactive map + gamification) but a cautionary one on the neutrality/credibility axis.

## 3. Patterns that work

- **One clean, quotable, chart-ready number per clip** (Harry Enten's segments) — built to be screenshotted and shared out of context without becoming misleading.
- **A recognizable presenter + prop/quirk** (Kornacki's khakis and big board; Nico Agosta's direct-to-camera "data dump") turns dry data into a memorable personality.
- **On-screen source citations** (FEC/OpenSecrets/Quiver Quantitative overlaid in-video) signal credibility inside the format itself, with no click-through required.
- **Shareable, personalized interactive tools** (270toWin's build-your-own map, Integrity Index's zip-code lookup and mini-game, Split Ticket/VoteHub trackers) convert passive viewers into distributors.
- **Free headline, paid depth** (Silver Bulletin, Strength In Numbers, Cook Political Report) — the number everyone wants stays public; the differentiated analysis is what's monetized.
- **A recurring, news-independent data drop** (Strength In Numbers' monthly Verasight poll; Silver Bulletin's near-daily model refresh) manufactures a reason to return even in slow news weeks.
- **One fixed weekly appointment** (The Downballot's Thursday podcast) builds habit better than irregular posting.
- **Fan-out of one number across formats** (newsletter → podcast → thread → carousel → clip) — Cook Political Report, Strength In Numbers and Split Ticket all repackage the same underlying analysis rather than producing separate content per channel.
- **Distribution partnerships** (Split Ticket × The Argument; Kalshi's CNBC/CNN/Fox data-feed deals) extend reach without diluting the core brand's own channels.
- **Visible "showing your work" as the trust mechanic** (V Spehar's explicit response to credibility skeptics; Enten's careful scoping of every stat) — a casual tone is forgiven when sourcing is visible.
- **Curiosity-gap hooks over neutral titles** — framing a post as a question about whether a wave election is forming, or as "every scenario mapped," consistently outperforms generic forecast-update framing.
- **Institutional/academic framing reads as neutral** (Sabato's Crystal Ball's UVA affiliation, Cook's 40-year history) even while operating on the same social platforms as partisan accounts.

## 4. Pitfalls

- **Undisclosed paid promotion.** Polymarket/Kalshi's influencer payments (up to ~$500/post) drew a US Senator's inquiry letter and a BBB/NAD referral for misleading, sometimes election-integrity-undermining claims — direct validation of Matteo's existing organic-only, no-boosting rule.
- **Advocacy dressed as neutral analysis.** Integrity Index is a registered PAC whose own output skews to one party; presenting that as objective "accountability" data invites exactly the bias critique it got publicly. Matteo's forecast must stay visibly distinct from this pattern.
- **Overclaiming certainty.** Prediction-market marketing has billed itself as the single most accurate way to track an election, a hard overclaim; better to show the number alongside its uncertainty and next to other estimates, as Matteo's plan (forecast next to poll average/market/Cook rating) already does.
- **Shipping visible broken features.** Integrity Index's own scorecards page showed a raw feature-flag error when checked — a small thing that instantly undercuts trust in a credibility-dependent product.
- **Engagement doesn't transfer across channels.** A huge TikTok following (hundreds of thousands) coexisted with single-digit engagement on the same organization's Substack post — don't assume growth on one platform predicts another.
- **Mixing fundraising asks into every data post.** Integrity Index's donate/merch/pledge CTAs embedded in explainer content blur "accountability tool" with "fundraising operation."
- **Volume/reaction-driven formats don't fit a once-daily, approved-by-one-person workflow.** Mockler's and Parnas's multiple-posts-per-day model works for commentary creators but is structurally incompatible with Matteo's plan of one carefully vetted post per day.
- **Self-reported reach claims.** Decision Desk HQ's claimed hundreds of millions of annual impressions on race calls, and similar figures from these organizations, are self-reported, not third-party audited — useful as a directional signal only.
