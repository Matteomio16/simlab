### Lessons

- **The disclosure gap, not the AI itself, caused the worst hit.** Aaru's Axios episode blew up because a downstream journalist dropped the AI-simulated-data context, not because Aaru itself claimed to be a real poll. Since the house rule already puts "AI-simulated voters, not a poll" inside every image, also repeat it in on-platform captions/alt text, since captions get cropped or stripped when reshared.
- **Point predictions followed by after-the-fact margin-of-error spin read badly.** Aaru called the 2024 race for Harris and then downplayed the loss as statistically close, which was mocked. Publish calibrated probabilities and a pre-committed scoring rule (e.g., Brier score by pilot race) *before* results are known, not a post-hoc defense after.
- **Grand claims get quoted back as evidence of hype** (e.g., Aaru's claim about simulating the entire globe within two years, singled out in the Silver Bulletin piece). Stick to the specific, falsifiable claims already in hand — right on direction, wrong on magnitude, shared blind spots, outlet-name bias, statistics beating models on levels — these read as careful rather than promotional.
- **Standards bodies and the harshest critic agree on the same fix: augment, don't replace, and always label.** Since this project already reconciles the simulation against real polls weekly, that hybrid design is the strongest point of difference from the pure silicon-sampling firms that AAPOR and Silver Bulletin are criticizing — lead with it explicitly, don't bury it.
- **Proactive, specific self-criticism reads as more credible than confidence.** Aaru's newer distrust-us pitch and Expected Parrot's prototyping-only framing both land better than the original hype cycle. Treat the six-model blind-spot findings as headline content, and keep publishing where the simulation was wrong week to week, not only where it was right.
- **A credible outside name on governance helps** — Electric Twin's ethics-board hire reads as a legitimacy move worth noting, though it's not something a solo project can replicate; the low-cost equivalent is publishing methodology and error logs openly.

### Formats that transfer to us

- **Show every simulated future as a mark, not just one probability.** Adopt the 538/50+1 approach of drawing one dot per simulated timeline for the Monte Carlo output in each pilot race — it fits the existing house rule that charts are made in code, and it makes "AI-simulated voters, not a poll" visually obvious rather than just a caption line.
- **Publish one clean chart on a fixed cadence, not long threads.** Epoch AI's single-topic Data Insight format suggests a short daily/weekly chart plus a tight caption will get cited and embedded more than a long explainer thread.
- **Make the misses part of the content, not an afterthought.** AI Village and Claude Plays Pokémon both get their staying power from visible, ongoing struggle, not only wins. The planned findings (right on direction, wrong on magnitude, shared blind spots, outlet-name bias, stats beating models on levels) are exactly this kind of content — publish them as the story, weekly, using OH/NC/TX as the running example.
- **Give people a running scoreboard to check.** LMArena's draw is a leaderboard that updates and that the industry itself cites. Consider a simple running scorecard ranking the six AI models against each other and against the statistical baseline in the three pilot races, updated on the same cadence as the daily posts.
- **One strong, named explainer invites debate content from others.** AI 2027's spread came partly from being specific and contestable enough that other commentators wrote responses. A single, clearly argued methods page (with limits stated up front) can seed that same secondary discussion.
- **Turn raw agent output into a shareable artifact, carefully.** AI Village and Project Sid both repackaged raw logs/transcripts as content. A short, clearly labeled weekly recap of what the simulated voter agents said could work the same way — but must stay unambiguous that it is synthetic text, never presented as a real quote from a real person.

## Scope C — 2026 platform facts

**Instagram**
- Reels reach roughly 2x+ more non-followers than carousels or single images; carousels win on saves/engagement among existing followers — directionally consistent across multiple 2026 analytics write-ups, but treat exact multipliers as **[unverified]** (marketing-analytics sources, not platform-official). [Metricool 2026](https://metricool.com/press-release-instagram-study-2026/)
- Meta reversed its 2024 stance: since a Jan 8, 2025 rollout, Instagram/Threads recommend political content by default (three-tier Less/Standard/More control, Standard = default), because Meta concluded that drawing a clean line around political content wasn't practical to maintain. A new, non-followed account posting political-data content can be recommended in 2026, unlike under the 2024–25 restricted period. [Engadget 1/8/25](https://www.engadget.com/social-media/threads-and-instagram-will-recommend-political-content-after-all-232343107.html)
- "AI info"/"AI-generated profile" labels mainly target AI-depicted people: as of Aug 31, 2026 Instagram limits reach for profiles featuring an undisclosed AI-generated person; using AI only to edit photos, polish captions, or make graphics does not require the label. Since house rules already bar AI images of candidates and keep charts code-generated, this project sits outside the label's main trigger — but any depiction of a simulated voter as a person could invite it. [TechCrunch 8/31/26](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/31/instagram-puts-new-limits-on-undisclosed-ai-profiles/)

**Threads**
- Reported at 400–500M monthly active users and ~150M DAU by mid-2026, with growth said to have overtaken X in mobile DAU in early 2026 — figures vary by source and should be treated as approximate **[unverified precise figures]**. **X**
- X open-sourced its recommendation algorithm on GitHub in Jan 2026 (Musk announced Jan 10; code published ~Jan 11; pledged updates every 4 weeks). [Bloomberg 1/10/26](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-10/elon-musk-says-x-to-make-its-algorithm-open-source-in-seven-days), [TechCrunch 1/20/26](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/20/x-open-sources-its-algorithm-while-facing-a-transparency-fine-and-grok-controversies)

- Practical takeaway: post charts/video natively; put the link (notapoll.org, or labs.scaliastudio.dev/midterms) in a reply rather than the main post.

**Bluesky**
- Roughly 43.5–46M registered users by Apr–Aug 2026 (from ~10M in late 2024), with an estimated 1.5–3M daily actives (~8–9% of registered users). [SocialPilot 2026](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/bluesky-statistics), [Proxidize 2026](https://proxidize.com/blog/bluesky-user-count-2026/)
- Skews young and male but broadening: 18–24 is still the largest single age band (~30%), though under-35 share fell from ~70% to ~62% over the past year; male users ~61.9% as of Feb 2026, down from a 70/30 split in late 2024.
- Community-run "starter packs" and feeds exist specifically for this audience — e.g. "Polls and Elections," "Polling Data," "US Political News," "2026 Elections" packs — a low-cost, direct way to reach election-data followers rather than relying on the general algorithm. [blueskystarterpack.com](https://blueskystarterpack.com/2026-elections)
- No universal policy of limiting political-content recommendations to non-followers was found for Bluesky (discovery runs through opt-in custom feeds rather than one central recommender) — **[inferred, not an explicit stated policy]**.

**TikTok**
- Paid political advertising and paid political content are banned outright for candidates/parties/PACs — not relevant operationally here since the project never pays for promotion, but relevant if any paid-placement offer ever comes in. [TikTok ads policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-politics-government-and-elections)
- Synthetic media depicting real candidates/officials saying things they didn't say is banned within 90 days of an election — already satisfied by the no-AI-candidate-images rule. [TikTok Integrity & Authenticity guidelines](https://www.tiktok.com/community-guidelines/en/integrity-authenticity)
- No rule specifically restricting foreign-based creators' *organic, unpaid* political commentary was found; the foreign-related restrictions located apply to state-affiliated media accounts' recommendation eligibility and cross-border ad spend, not independent creators. **[possible gap in my search, not a confirmed absence — worth checking TikTok's current guidelines directly before the Oct 12 launch]**. [TikTok Newsroom](https://newsroom.tiktok.com/en-us/updating-our-policies-for-political-accounts)
- Worth adding: short vertical video matches the planned Reels format, and TikTok's discovery is generally considered friendly to brand-new accounts (not follower-gated), which suits a fresh account.

**YouTube / YouTube Shorts**
- Political-ad rules (identity/location verification, on-screen "Paid for by" disclosure) are not relevant since the project runs no ads.
- Organic creator content must carry a synthetic/altered-media disclosure label for realistic AI election content; it displays in the Shorts player and description regardless of the creator's identity, viewpoint, or language — again, largely avoided by the no-AI-candidate-imagery rule, but would apply if any dramatized voter-agent visual/voice content is ever produced. [Policy summary, 2026](https://minimatters.com/youtube-altered-or-synthetic-content-disclosure/)
- No foreign-creator-specific organic-content restriction was found beyond the general foreign-national funding ban on paid political ads and an EU-specific political-ad declaration requirement (from Apr 2026, EU-targeted campaigns only) — **[unverified beyond that]**.
- Worth adding as a secondary, cross-posted channel for the same vertical charts; new-channel discovery on Shorts is generally slower than TikTok's, so treat as tertiary rather than a launch-day priority.
